Here's what I'm thinking for the half-day I'd run for your team.

What I took from our conversation: around 50 people, most working with data and investment analysis every day, and opinion on AI split right across the room. Some of your analysts are already keen, some sit on the fence, and part of the senior team doesn't trust it yet, so we want to build that trust in the session.

The centrepiece is a game called Shark Tank: AI Edition. I split the room into mixed teams, seasoned investors sitting with junior analysts, and give each team a fund with $10m to deploy. Four impact companies are pitching for the money. Using AI, the teams get 90 minutes to work out which businesses are sound and which are hiding a problem in their numbers, then decide who to back and how much.

The catch is the clock. There's no way through four decks, their financials and a founder interview by hand in that time, so the only way to finish is to put AI to work. A couple of the companies look brilliant but are quietly bleeding money. One is the real deal. The teams that prompt well and check the answers beat the teams that trust the machine, and that's the whole lesson. It's the one your sceptics will walk out believing: AI is fast, but you stay the expert, and you verify.

Everything runs on invented companies and made-up numbers, so nothing sensitive goes near a chatbot.

How the half-day runs

  1. A quick temperature check to see where the room stands on AI. 20–30 min
  2. Two or three live demos of AI workflows, the kind of work your team actually does, to show what's possible. 20–30 min
  3. A short session on compliant AI. if useful, agreed with Dryden
  4. Shark Tank: AI Edition. 90 min
  5. A short break, then each team pitches its decisions to the panel and I reveal what every company was hiding.
  6. A closing session on how to think about AI in your work: where it helps, where a human stays in charge, and how to use it responsibly here. 20–30 min
  7. Open Q&A with me. 30 min

What your team walks away with: everyone has now used AI on realistic investment work rather than a toy demo, you'll have a simple safe-use framework the whole firm can follow, and you'll have real movement across that divide you described, because the sceptics will have spent the afternoon catching the AI's mistakes on their own turf. That's what turns a doubter round.

Everyone brings a laptop and uses whatever AI tools they like. I bring the entire simulation ready to run.
